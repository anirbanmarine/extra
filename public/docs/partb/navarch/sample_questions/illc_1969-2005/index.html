      Under the International Convention on Load Lines, 1966 (as modified by the 1988 Protocol), the tabular freeboard is only the starting value. The final assigned summer freeboard is obtained by applying prescribed corrections and deductions that reflect hull form, depth, reserve buoyancy, strength, weathertight integrity, and the amount of enclosed superstructure. imorules

      Q1. Corrections to tabulated freeboard

      Starting point

      1. Determine the ship’s type:

        • Type A: usually tankers with high inherent weathertight integrity, small deck openings and good subdivision.
        • Type B: all ships that do not meet Type A conditions—most dry-cargo ships, container ships, bulk carriers and general-cargo ships.

      2. From the appropriate Type A or Type B table, obtain the tabular freeboard corresponding to the convention length LLL.

      3. Apply the relevant corrections in the specified order.

      A typical calculation sequence for a Type B ship is:

      [ \boxed{ F_{\text{assigned}}

      F_{\text{tabular}} \pm C_{L<100} \pm C_{C_B} \pm C_D \pm C_{\text{deck line}}

      • D_{\text{superstructure/trunk}} \pm C_{\text{sheer}} } ]

      Then verify:

      Fassigned≥Fminimum F_{\text{assigned}} \geq F_{\text{minimum}} Fassigned​≥Fminimum​

      and also check the separate minimum bow-height requirement.

      Main corrections and their principles

      Correction / deduction Effect on freeboard Underlying principle
      Correction for ships under 100 m Usually increases Type B freeboard when the ship has little enclosed superstructure A short dry-cargo ship with limited enclosed volume has less reserve buoyancy and is more affected by waves; it needs greater protection than the basic table alone provides
      Block-coefficient correction Increase if CB>0.68C_B > 0.68CB​>0.68; reduce if CB<0.68C_B < 0.68CB​<0.68, subject to limits A fuller ship has less buoyancy recovery as it heels or trims, greater exposure to green seas, and generally needs more reserve buoyancy. A finer ship has more favourable form characteristics and may receive a reduction
      Depth correction Increase where actual depth exceeds the tabular/reference depth; may reduce where it is less, within limits Greater moulded depth gives more hull volume above the loaded waterline. The Convention gives credit for this reserve buoyancy, but only to a controlled extent and subject to minimum-freeboard rules
      Correction for deck-line position Adjusts freeboard if the deck line is not at the normal intersection of freeboard deck and shell Freeboard is measured from the upper edge of the deck line. If the deck line is shifted from its normal datum, the numerical freeboard must be corrected so that the actual load line still represents the intended reserve buoyancy
      Superstructure and trunk deduction Reduces freeboard Efficient enclosed superstructures and qualifying trunks create protected enclosed buoyancy above the freeboard deck, improve protection of deck openings, and reduce the likelihood of downflooding
      Sheer correction Excess sheer reduces freeboard; deficiency increases it Sheer provides extra height, reserve buoyancy and protection at the ends, especially forward where green-sea boarding is severe. Deficient sheer removes this protection
      Minimum bow-height check May require an increase in freeboard or bow height Adequate bow freeboard is needed to reduce deck wetness, green-water loading, hatch-cover damage and progressive flooding risk
      Minimum freeboard check Prevents excessive reduction Regardless of deductions, the final assigned freeboard cannot be less than the Convention minimum applicable to the ship

      The Convention chapter on freeboards specifically includes corrections for ships under 100 m, block coefficient, depth, deck-line position, superstructures/trunks, sheer, minimum bow height and minimum freeboards. imorules

      Block coefficient correction

      The block coefficient is:

      CB=∇LBT C_B=\frac{\nabla}{LBT} CB​=LBT∇​

      where:

      • ∇\nabla∇ = moulded displacement volume,
      • LLL = Convention length,
      • BBB = moulded breadth,
      • TTT = relevant moulded draught.

      For a Type B ship, the standard reference form is approximately:

      CB=0.68 C_B=0.68 CB​=0.68

      The usual correction principle is:

      CCB∝(CB−0.68) C_{C_B} \propto (C_B-0.68) CCB​​∝(CB​−0.68)

      Therefore:

      • CB>0.68C_B>0.68CB​>0.68: add freeboard.
      • CB<0.68C_B<0.68CB​<0.68: deduct freeboard, subject to the permitted maximum deduction.

      A full-bodied bulk carrier, for example, normally attracts an increase because it is more vulnerable to deck immersion and may have less favourable reserve-buoyancy characteristics than a finer vessel.

      Depth correction

      The relevant depth is the freeboard depth, measured amidships from keel to the top of the freeboard deck beam at side, including the thickness of the freeboard deck at side where applicable. assets.publishing.service.gov

      A deeper ship possesses more potential enclosed buoyancy above the assigned waterline. Thus, subject to Convention limits:

      • Extra depth can justify a freeboard deduction.
      • Inadequate depth requires an addition.

      However, additional depth alone must not be used to claim unsafe reductions where the vessel lacks proper structural strength, effective enclosure, closing appliances, or adequate stability. The Convention expressly assumes adequate structural strength, stability and avoidance of excessive structural stress at the assigned draught. assets.publishing.service.gov

      Superstructure deduction

      An enclosed superstructure is valuable because it creates reserve buoyancy that remains available when the vessel is deeply laden or heeled.

      The deduction depends principally on:

      EL \frac{E}{L} LE​

      where:

      • EEE = total effective length of enclosed superstructures and qualifying trunks,
      • LLL = Convention length.

      The more of the vessel’s length covered by effective enclosed superstructure, the greater the permitted deduction. If effective superstructure length equals the full ship length, the maximum applicable deduction is available; if it covers only part of the length, the deduction is proportionately smaller. laws-lois.justice.gc

      Sheer correction

      Sheer is treated separately because a raised deck line at the ends gives real safety benefit, especially in waves. The Convention correction is based on the difference between actual and standard sheer. A shortage of sheer generally increases freeboard; excess sheer may reduce it. assets.publishing.service.gov

      Q2. Sheer: definition and calculation

      Definition

      Sheer is the longitudinal curvature of the freeboard deck measured at the ship’s side. It is the vertical distance between the actual deck line at side and a reference line drawn parallel to the keel through the sheer line amidships.

      In simple terms:

      • The deck is lowest around midships.
      • It rises toward the bow and usually toward the stern.
      • The upward rise is called sheer.

      The Convention measures sheer from the deck at side to a reference line parallel to the keel through the amidship sheer line. assets.publishing.service.gov

      [ \text{Sheer correction}

      How sheer improves safety and stability

      Strictly speaking, sheer is primarily a reserve-buoyancy, seaworthiness and downflooding-protection feature rather than an initial-stability feature such as GMGMGM.

      It contributes to safety in the following ways:

      • Increases hull volume above the loaded waterline at the bow and stern.
      • Increases reserve buoyancy as the end immerses due to waves, trim or heel.
      • Improves longitudinal restoring characteristics in pitching and trimming.
      • Reduces frequency of bow immersion and deck wetness.
      • Helps protect hatch covers, vents, doors and deck machinery from green-water impacts.
      • Reduces the likelihood of water accumulating in exposed wells or entering deck openings.
      • Improves the residual buoyancy margin at the ends after limited flooding.

      Sheer does not usually create a large change in initial transverse GMGMGM, but it materially improves reserve buoyancy, the range of stability, resistance to deck immersion, longitudinal behaviour in waves, and protection against downflooding.

      Limit on total deduction or addition

      The assigned freeboard cannot be reduced without limit. The final value remains subject to:

      • Minimum-freeboard provisions.
      • Minimum bow-height requirements.
      • Adequate strength and intact stability.
      • Full compliance with the conditions of assignment: hatch closures, doors, ventilators, air pipes, freeing ports, guard rails, machinery-space protection, cargo ports and other opening protections.

      The Convention allows a limited reduction from tabular freeboard when the vessel earns credit for favourable form, depth, enclosed superstructure, trunks and excess sheer; conversely, a vessel with unfavourable form, inadequate depth, deficient sheer or inadequate superstructure receives additions. The final assigned freeboard must never be below the prescribed minimum. imorules

      Q3. Effective length of superstructure

      Definition

      The effective length of superstructure, denoted by EEE, is the length of an enclosed superstructure that is accepted for freeboard-deduction purposes.

      It may be less than the actual geometrical length SSS, because the superstructure earns credit only to the extent that it provides meaningful enclosed buoyancy and weather protection.

      E=S=80 m E=S=80\ \text{m} E=S=80 m

      Why effective length is used

      The reason is that not all structures of equal plan length give equal reserve buoyancy.

      A tall, fully enclosed, strongly built, weathertight superstructure:

      • Adds real enclosed buoyancy.
      • Remains effective when the vessel heels.
      • Delays downflooding.
      • Protects openings on the freeboard deck.
      • Improves survivability in waves and damaged conditions.

      A low or poorly enclosed deckhouse may provide less useful buoyancy and may be immersed or downflooded sooner. It should therefore not receive the same freeboard deduction.

      Exam-ready summary

      • Tabular freeboard is selected from the Type A or Type B table using Convention length.
      • It is adjusted for: ship length below 100 m, block coefficient, depth, deck-line position, enclosed superstructures/trunks and sheer.
      • It is then checked against the minimum freeboard and minimum bow-height requirements.
      • Sheer is the rise of the freeboard deck at side from midships to the ends; it improves reserve buoyancy, reduces deck wetness and gives protection against green seas and downflooding.
      • Excess sheer earns a deduction; deficient sheer requires an addition.
      • Effective superstructure length is not necessarily the actual length: a low superstructure earns only proportionate credit because it supplies less protected buoyancy.
      • For a normal enclosed superstructure below standard height:
      E=SHHs \boxed{E=S\frac{H}{H_s}} E=SHs​H​​
      • The greater the effective length EEE, the greater the permitted deduction from tabular freeboard—subject always to minimum safety limits.
