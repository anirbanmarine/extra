      Ship Waves and Wave Theory

      Relative Motion and Period of Encounter

      Vectors:

      • Ship’s speed vector: V⃗s\vec{V}_sVs​
      • Wave speed vector (phase velocity/Celerity): V⃗w\vec{V}_wVw​
      • Angle between them: μ\muμ

      Key Concepts:

      • Absolute period of waves: TwT_wTw​ (true wave period)
      • Period of encounter: TeT_eTe​ (apparent period relative to ship)
      • TeT_eTe​ is the resultant of TwT_wTw​ and the ship’s speed through waves

      Direction Convention:

      • Head seas (meeting waves): μ=180∘\mu = 180^\circμ=180∘
      • Following seas: μ=0∘\mu = 0^\circμ=0∘

      Relationships:

      Te∝Vs(ship speed)T_e \propto V_s \quad \text{(ship speed)}Te​∝Vs​(ship speed)

      Te∝μ(encounter angle)T_e \propto \mu \quad \text{(encounter angle)}Te​∝μ(encounter angle)

      Wave Group Velocity:

      Vgroup=12Vw(waves travel at half phase speed)V_{group} = \frac{1}{2}V_w \quad \text{(waves travel at half phase speed)}Vgroup​=21​Vw​(waves travel at half phase speed)

      Note: A group of waves travels at Vw/2V_w/2Vw​/2, while individual wave crests travel at VwV_wVw​

      Energy Distribution: Exactly half the energy of a wave is kinetic and half is potential.

      Maximum Wave Height:

      Hw-max≈1.6×SWH(over 10 minutes)H_{w\text{-max}} \approx 1.6 \times \text{SWH} \quad \text{(over 10 minutes)}Hw-max​≈1.6×SWH(over 10 minutes)

      (Note: The document also mentions Hw-max=2×SWHH_{w\text{-max}} = 2 \times \text{SWH}Hw-max​=2×SWH in another section—verify which coefficient your course uses)

      Trochoidal Wave Theory

      Historical Background

      • 1905: Froude theorized that irregular wave systems are composites of many regular wave systems
      • 1952: Researchers such as Trucker applied Fourier analysis to regular wave theory based on trochoidal waveforms
      • The Trochoidal wave theory is not a perfect replica of observed sea states but comes closest compared to other theories

      Definition

      A Trochoid is a curve traced by a fixed point inside a circle as the circle rolls along and under a straight line.

      Geometric Properties

      λ=Lw=2πR(where R = radius of rolling circle))\lambda = L_w = 2\pi R \quad \text{(where \(R\) = radius of rolling circle)})λ=Lw​=2πR(where R = radius of rolling circle))

      hw=2r(wave height, where r = radius of small circle)h_w = 2r \quad \text{(wave height, where \(r\) = radius of small circle)} hw​=2r(wave height, where r = radius of small circle)

      Physical Characteristics

      • Waveform has sharper crests than troughs
      • Theory assumes water has zero viscosity and is frictionless
      • Only the waveform (phase) moves, not the water mass
      • Water particles move in closed circular orbits (one revolution per wave period)
      • At depths greater than λ2\frac{\lambda}{2}2λ​, there is no water particle movement (wave base)

      Amplitude Decay with Depth:

      r=r0⋅e−(2πzλ)r = r_0 \cdot e^{-\left(\frac{2\pi z}{\lambda}\right)}r=r0​⋅e−(λ2πz​)

      (where zzz = depth below surface)

      Assumptions for Simple Wave Theory

      1. Crests are straight, parallel, and equally spaced
      2. Wave heights remain constant
      3. Wave form advances perpendicular to crest lines with uniform velocity VwV_wVw​
      4. VwV_wVw​ is “Celerity” (phase velocity), not individual particle velocity
      5. Water has zero compressibility and zero viscosity
      6. Trochoidal waves occur in deep waters
      7. Deep sea waves: half kinetic, half potential energy
      8. Individual waves rise behind the “dead water zone,” travel through the group at 2×Vw2 \times V_w2×Vw​ (relative to group), then subside in the dead water zone ahead
      9. Waves travel in groups

      Significant Wave Height (SWH)

      Definition: When wave heights hwh_whw​ are arranged in descending order, the mean of the highest one-third of the waves is called SWH.

      Requirements:

      • At least 20 observations needed for reliable assessment
      • Waves not fully developed are excluded

      Importance:

      • Vital for master’s decisions in heavy weather (particularly quartering/following seas)
      • MSC 1228 specifically mentions importance of SWH observation
