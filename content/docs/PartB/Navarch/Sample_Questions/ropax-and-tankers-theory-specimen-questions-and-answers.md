## **① Inherent Strength of Design: Oil Tanker vs. Dry Cargo Ship**

### **A. Structural Strength Advantages of Tankers**

#### **1. Double Hull Design (MARPOL Annex I, Reg. 13)**
- **Tankers**:
  - **Mandatory double hull** (or double bottom + double sides) for oil tankers **> 5,000 DWT** (post-**Exxon Valdez**, 1989).
  - **Reduces oil spill risk** in grounding/collision.
  - **Increases structural redundancy** (two layers of hull plating).

- **Dry Cargo Ships**:
  - Typically **single hull** (except bulk carriers > 150m, which may have double sides for ballast tanks).

#### **2. Longitudinal Strength**
- **Tankers**:
  - **Continuous longitudinal bulkheads** (centerline or side) **reduce hogging/sagging stresses**.
  - **Cargo tanks act as structural members**, contributing to **longitudinal strength**.
  - **Higher section modulus** (due to deep, box-like tanks).

- **Dry Cargo Ships**:
  - **Open cargo holds** (no longitudinal bulkheads) → **lower section modulus**.
  - **More prone to hogging/sagging** (especially in bulk carriers with large, empty holds).

#### **3. Transverse Strength**
- **Tankers**:
  - **Transverse bulkheads** (every 2–4 tanks) **resist racking stresses**.
  - **Wing tanks and double bottoms** add **transverse rigidity**.

- **Dry Cargo Ships**:
  - **Fewer transverse bulkheads** (typically at 1/5 ship length intervals).
  - **Higher racking stresses** in open holds.

#### **4. Fatigue Resistance**
- **Tankers**:
  - **Smoother stress distribution** due to **liquid cargo** (no point loads).
  - **Corrosion protection** (coatings, cathodic protection) **mandatory** in cargo tanks.

- **Dry Cargo Ships**:
  - **Point loads** from **grain, ore, or containers** → **higher fatigue stress concentrations**.
  - **Corrosion in cargo holds** (e.g., bulk carriers carrying salt, ore, or coal).

#### **5. Stability Advantages**
- **Tankers**:
  - **Lower KG (Vertical Center of Gravity)**: Liquid cargo (oil) fills tanks from the bottom up → **lower VCG**.
  - **Free surface effect minimized** (tanks are subdivided).
  - **Higher GM (Metacentric Height)** → **stiffer ship** (less rolling).

- **Dry Cargo Ships**:
  - **Higher KG**: Granular cargo (e.g., grain) can shift, raising VCG.
  - **Free surface effect** in partially filled holds.
  - **Lower GM** → **tender ship** (more rolling).


---

### **B. Freeboard Assignment Under ILLC (International Load Line Convention, 1966)**
- **Tankers**:
  - **Assigned lesser freeboard** than dry cargo ships of the **same dimensions** because:
    1. **Lower KG** → **higher stability** (less risk of capsizing).
    2. **Double hull** → **better damage survivability** (reduces risk of flooding).
    3. **Subdivision** (tanks act as watertight compartments) → **better buoyancy after damage**.
    4. **ILLC Type B Freeboard**: Tankers are classified as **Type B** (lower freeboard) due to **higher stability and subdivision**.

- **Dry Cargo Ships**:
  - **Assigned higher freeboard** (Type A or B-60) due to:
    - **Higher KG** (less stable).
    - **Open cargo holds** → **higher risk of flooding** if damaged.
    - **No subdivision in cargo holds** → **less damage survivability**.

## **② Freeboard: Tanker vs. Bulk Carrier (Same Dimensions)**

### **A. Why Tankers Have Lesser Freeboard**
1. **Stability**:
   - **Tankers**: **Lower KG** (liquid cargo) → **higher GM** → **more stable**.
   - **Bulk Carriers**: **Higher KG** (granular cargo) → **lower GM** → **less stable**.

2. **Subdivision & Damage Survivability**:
   - **Tankers**:
     - **Double hull** → **reduces flooding risk**.
     - **Cargo tanks are watertight** → **limits progressive flooding**.
   - **Bulk Carriers**:
     - **Single hull** → **higher flooding risk** if damaged.
     - **Open cargo holds** → **no subdivision** → **faster flooding**.

3. **ILLC Classification**:
   - **Tankers**: **Type B** (lower freeboard).
   - **Bulk Carriers**: **Type A** (higher freeboard).


---

### **B. Role of Pump Room Double Bottom & Cargo Pumps in Survivability**
1. **Pump Room Double Bottom (MARPOL Annex I, Reg. 13G)**
   - **Mandatory for tankers** > 5,000 DWT.
   - **Prevents oil leakage** in case of **grounding or collision**.
   - **Acts as a secondary barrier** → **improves damage stability**.

2. **Cargo Pumps**
   - **Located in pump rooms** (separate from cargo tanks).
   - **Submersible pumps** (in each tank) **reduce risk of oil spill** in case of hull breach.
   - **Remote-controlled valves** → **faster emergency shutdown**.

3. **Survivability Benefits**:
   - **Reduces risk of oil pollution** (environmental protection).
   - **Enhances damage stability** (fewer compartments flooded).
   - **Compliance with SOLAS II-2 (Fire Safety)** and **MARPOL Annex I**.

## **③ Stability Failures in Al Salaam Boccaccio 98 ROPAX (2006)**

### **A. Incident Overview**
- **Date**: 3 February 2006.
- **Location**: Red Sea (near Safaga, Egypt).
- **Casualties**: ~1,000 deaths (one of the deadliest maritime disasters in peacetime).
- **Ship Type**: ROPAX (Roll-on/Roll-off Passenger Ferry).
- **Cause**: **Fire + Stability Failure** (capsizing due to loss of transverse stability).


---

### **B. Stability Failures (With Diagrams)**

#### **(a) Fire Fighting Operations**
- **Fire Origin**: Engine room (electrical fault or fuel leak).
- **Spread**: Fire traveled through **ventilation ducts** to **car deck and passenger areas**.
- **Stability Impact**:
  - **Water used for firefighting** accumulated on **car deck** (not drained properly).
  - **Free surface effect** → **loss of GM** → **increased rolling**.
  - **Asymmetrical flooding** (water on one side) → **heeling moment**.

**Diagram**:
```
  +---------------------+
  |   Passenger Deck    |  ← Fire spreads upward
  +---------------------+
  |   Car Deck          |  ← Water accumulates here (free surface)
  +---------------------+
  |   Engine Room       |  ← Fire origin
  +---------------------+
```
- **Result**: **Heeling → Capsizing**.


---

#### **(b) Passengers Running to One Side**
- **Human Behavior**: Passengers **rushed to one side** (starboard) to escape fire.
- **Stability Impact**:
  - **Sudden shift of KG** to starboard → **large heeling moment**.
  - **Reduced GM** → **ship became unstable**.

**Diagram**:
```
  Before:          After:
  +----+            +----+
  |    |            |    |← Passengers
  | KG |            | KG→|  rush here
  +----+            +----+
  △ GM △             △ GM→0 (Unstable)
```


---

#### **(c) Wide Turn at Speed**
- **Maneuvering Error**: Ship was **making a wide turn at high speed** (15+ knots).
- **Stability Impact**:
  - **Centrifugal force** → **outward heeling moment**.
  - **Combined with fire water and passenger shift** → **excessive heel angle**.
  - **Loss of transverse stability** → **capsizing**.

**Diagram**:
```
  +---------------------+
  |       Turn          |← Centrifugal force → Heel
  |   (High Speed)      |
  +---------------------+
  △ Heel Angle Increases △
```


---

### **C. Root Causes of Stability Failure**
1. **Poor Subdivision**: **No watertight bulkheads** on the car deck → **flooding spread rapidly**.
2. **Inadequate Drainage**: **No scuppers or pumps** on the car deck → **water accumulated**.
3. **High KG**: **Passenger decks and car decks** were **high above the keel** → **low GM**.
4. **No Damage Stability Compliance**: **Did not meet SOLAS-90 standards**.

## **④ SOLAS-90 Standard for ROPAX Ships**

### **A. SOLAS-90 Standard**
- **Applies to**: ROPAX ships (Roll-on/Roll-off Passenger Ships) built **after 1 July 1990**.
- **Key Requirements**:
  1. **Damage Stability**:
     - **Must survive flooding of any single compartment** (including car deck).
     - **Final GM ≥ 0.05 m** after damage.
     - **Maximum heel angle ≤ 15°** in damaged condition.
  2. **Subdivision**:
     - **Watertight bulkheads** must extend to the **freeboard deck**.
     - **Car decks** must have **watertight doors** (automatically closed in case of fire/flooding).
  3. **Fire Safety**:
     - **Fire-resistant divisions** (A-60 class) around **car decks and accommodation**.
     - **Fixed fire-extinguishing systems** (e.g., water mist, CO₂).


---

### **B. SOLAS-90+ (50) Standard**
- **Definition**: An **enhanced version** of SOLAS-90, introduced after the **Estonia disaster (1994)**.
- **Key Improvements**:
  1. **Increased Damage Stability**:
     - **Must survive flooding of any two adjacent compartments** (not just one).
     - **Final GM ≥ 0.10 m** (higher than SOLAS-90).
  2. **Stricter Subdivision**:
     - **Additional watertight bulkheads** on car decks.
     - **Higher freeboard** (to reduce flooding risk).
  3. **Enhanced Fire Safety**:
     - **Automatic sprinklers** in car decks.
     - **Improved ventilation control** (fire dampers).


---

### **C. Stockholm Agreement (1996)**
- **Context**: After the **Estonia disaster (1994)**, the **IMO** and **EU** agreed on **stricter rules for ROPAX ships**.
- **Key Decisions**:
  1. **Retrofitting Existing Ships**:
     - **All ROPAX ships** (even pre-1990) must **comply with SOLAS-90+ (50) by 2010**.
  2. **A and Amax**:
     - **A**: **Permissible damage length** (maximum length of a single compartment that can flood without capsizing).
     - **Amax**: **Maximum allowable damage length** (based on ship’s dimensions and stability).
     - **Compliance Requirement**: **A ≤ 0.5 × Amax** (ship must survive flooding of **half the maximum allowable damage length**).
  3. **Lines of Defense**:
     - **Primary**: **Watertight bulkheads**.
     - **Secondary**: **Fire-resistant divisions**.
     - **Tertiary**: **Improved evacuation systems** (e.g., lifeboats on both sides).

## **⑥ Design Philosophy & Rulemaking for ROPAX Safety**

### **A. Multi-Pronged Approach to Safety**
The IMO and classification societies (e.g., DNV, LR, ABS) use a **layered approach** to improve ROPAX safety:

| **Layer** | **Objective** | **Examples (Rules & Regulations)** |
|-----------|---------------|------------------------------------|
| **1. Prevention** | Avoid accidents (fire, flooding, collision). | Fire-resistant materials (SOLAS II-2), Navigation equipment (SOLAS V), Training (STCW). |
| **2. Containment** | Limit damage if an accident occurs. | Watertight bulkheads (SOLAS II-1), Double hulls (MARPOL Annex I), Fire doors (SOLAS II-2). |
| **3. Mitigation** | Reduce consequences of accidents (e.g., flooding, fire). | Damage stability (SOLAS II-1, Reg. 8-4), Fixed fire extinguishing systems (SOLAS II-2, Reg. 10). |
| **4. Evacuation** | Ensure safe escape of passengers and crew. | Lifeboats on both sides (SOLAS III), Emergency lighting (SOLAS II-2, Reg. 13), Muster stations (SOLAS III). |
| **5. Survivability** | Improve chances of survival after an accident. | SOLAS-90+ (50) standards (enhanced damage stability), Stockholm Agreement (retrofitting). |


---

### **B. Rulemaking Process**
1. **Incident Analysis**: Investigate disasters (e.g., Estonia, Al Salaam Boccaccio 98) to identify weaknesses.
2. **Risk Assessment**: Formal Safety Assessment (FSA) to evaluate hazards and mitigation measures.
3. **Regulation Development**: IMO Sub-Committees (e.g., DE, FP, SDC) draft new rules. Class Societies provide technical guidelines.
4. **Implementation & Enforcement**: SOLAS amendments (e.g., SOLAS-90, SOLAS-90+ (50)). Port State Control (PSC) inspections to ensure compliance.

## **⑦ Fire Safety Design for Ro-Ro Ships (SOLAS Requirements)**

### **A. Definitions**
1. **Ro-Ro Space**:
   - A **cargo space** with **roll-on/roll-off access** (e.g., car decks, vehicle decks).
   - **Characteristics**: Large, open areas (no permanent subdivision). High fire risk (vehicles with fuel, batteries, etc.).
   - **SOLAS Definition**: "A space not normally subdivided in any way and extending to either the full width or the full length of the ship" (SOLAS II-2, Reg. 3.44).

2. **Special Category Space**:
   - A **cargo space** that is **not a Ro-Ro space** but has **similar fire risks** (e.g., spaces carrying dangerous goods, vehicles with fuel).
   - **SOLAS Definition**: "A space intended for the carriage of vehicles with fuel in their tanks for their own propulsion" (SOLAS II-2, Reg. 3.45).


---

### **B. Fire Safety Goals (SOLAS II-2, Part C)**

| **Goal** | **Ro-Ro Spaces** | **Special Category Spaces** |
|----------|------------------|-------------------------------|
| **Prevention** | No open flames in cargo areas. Hot work permits required. | Same as Ro-Ro spaces. No smoking in cargo areas. |
| **Detection** | Automatic fire detection (heat/smoke detectors). CCTV monitoring. | Same as Ro-Ro spaces. |
| **Containment** | A-60 class bulkheads around cargo spaces. Fire doors (A-60) on access points. | Same as Ro-Ro spaces. |
| **Extinction** | Fixed water-based fire-extinguishing system (e.g., water mist, sprinklers). Portable extinguishers (CO₂, dry chemical). | Fixed CO₂ or water mist system (for dangerous goods). Portable extinguishers. |
| **Structural Integrity** | Non-combustible materials (SOLAS II-2, Reg. 3). Thermal insulation to delay fire spread. | Same as Ro-Ro spaces. |
| **Ventilation Control** | Fire dampers in ventilation ducts. Automatic shutdown in case of fire. | Same as Ro-Ro spaces. |


---

### **C. SOLAS Requirements for Ro-Ro & Special Category Spaces**
1. **Fire Main System**: Hose reels and hydrants must cover **all Ro-Ro spaces**.
2. **Fixed Fire-Extinguishing Systems**:
   - **Water mist or sprinklers** (for Ro-Ro spaces).
   - **CO₂ or foam** (for special category spaces with flammable cargo).
3. **Fire Doors**:
   - **A-60 class** (fire-resistant for 60 minutes).
   - **Self-closing and remotely controlled**.
4. **Ventilation**:
   - **Fire dampers** in **all ventilation ducts** serving Ro-Ro spaces.
   - **Automatic closure** in case of fire.
5. **Structural Fire Protection**:
   - **Bulkheads and decks** around Ro-Ro spaces must be **A-60 class**.
   - **Insulation** to prevent **heat transfer** to adjacent spaces.

## **⑧ Corrosion in Tankers (or Bulk Carriers) & Regulatory Solutions**

### **A. Corrosion Problems in Tankers**

| **Area** | **Type of Corrosion** | **Causes** | **Impact** |
|----------|-----------------------|------------|------------|
| **1. Cargo Tanks** | General Corrosion (uniform thinning). | Sulfur in crude oil (forms H₂S, which reacts with steel). Water in cargo (electrolyte for corrosion). | Reduced plate thickness → structural failure. Leaks → oil pollution. |
| **2. Ballast Tanks** | Pitting Corrosion (localized deep pits). | Seawater (high chloride content). Poor coating maintenance. | Holes in tanks → flooding risk. Reduced structural strength. |
| **3. Double Bottom** | Crevice Corrosion (in gaps between plates). | Stagnant water in ballast tanks. Poor drainage. | Cracks in welds → leaks. Loss of watertight integrity. |
| **4. Deck & Superstructure** | Atmospheric Corrosion (rusting). | Salt spray (marine environment). Lack of paint maintenance. | Aesthetic damage. Weakened superstructure. |
| **5. Pipelines & Valves** | Erosion-Corrosion (flow-induced wear). | High-velocity oil/water flow. Abrasive particles (sand, scale). | Leaks in pipes → cargo loss/pollution. Valve failure. |


---

### **B. Regulatory Solutions to Corrosion**

| **Regulation** | **Requirement** | **Impact** |
|----------------|-----------------|------------|
| **1. MARPOL Annex I, Reg. 13G** | Double Hull for Tankers (>5,000 DWT). | Reduces oil spill risk in grounding/collision. Corrosion in one hull does not affect the other. |
| **2. SOLAS II-1, Reg. 3-11** | Corrosion Prevention in Ballast Tanks: Coatings (e.g., epoxy, zinc-rich). Cathodic Protection (sacrificial anodes or impressed current). | Extends tank life by 15–20 years. Reduces pitting corrosion. |
| **3. IACS UR Z10.4** | Enhanced Survey Requirements for Tankers: Close-up surveys every 5 years. Ultrasonic Thickness Measurements (UTM) for cargo/ballast tanks. | Early detection of corrosion. Prevents sudden structural failure. |
| **4. SOLAS II-2, Reg. 4.5.5** | Fire-Resistant Coatings in Cargo Tanks (for crude oil tankers). | Delays structural failure in case of fire. |
| **5. IMO PSPC (Performance Standard for Protective Coatings)** | Mandatory high-quality coatings for ballast tanks (resolution MSC.215(82)). | Reduces corrosion rate by 50–70%. |
| **6. Class Society Rules (e.g., ABS, DNV, LR)** | Corrosion Margins: Additional plate thickness (e.g., 2–3 mm extra in high-risk areas). | Compensates for expected corrosion over the ship’s life. |


---

### **C. Tanker Losses Due to Corrosion**

| **Ship Name** | **Year** | **Incident** | **Corrosion-Related Cause** |
|--------------|----------|--------------|-----------------------------|
| **1. Erika** | 1999 | Broke in two off Brittany, France → 400,000 tons of oil spilled. | Severe corrosion in cargo tanks (thickness reduced to 1–2 mm in some areas). Poor maintenance (no proper coatings). |
| **2. Prestige** | 2002 | Split in half off Spain → 77,000 tons of oil spilled. | Corrosion in single-hull design (no double hull). Brittle fracture due to low-temperature corrosion. |
| **3. Sea Empress** | 1996 | Grounded off Wales → 72,000 tons of oil spilled. | Corrosion in ballast tanks → structural weakness. Hull breach in grounding. |
| **4. MT Haven** | 1991 | Exploded and sank off Italy → 144,000 tons of oil spilled. | Corrosion in cargo tanks → leakage of volatile gases. Static electricity + corrosion → explosion. |
